LCA Brand and WordPress Site

A fictional charity called the Leopard Conservation Association (LCA) that works to stop leopard habitat loss. I designed its logo and poster series in Adobe Illustrator, then built its WordPress site, audited it against WCAG and rebuilt it in HTML and CSS with the problems fixed.

The problem

A charity relies on donations, but the target audience finds it hard to trust charity websites that don't feel genuine. The brand and the site had to come across as honest and open about the LCA's goals and plans.

My actions

I created the LCA's brand identity in Adobe Illustrator, including the logo and a poster series. I then built the site in WordPress, audited it for accessibility and rebuilt it in HTML and CSS. I defined a target audience and wrote a persona so the design met their needs.

Outcome

My accessibility audit found that the orange buttons failed WCAG contrast at 4.14:1 and that my images were missing alt text. In the HTML and CSS rebuild the buttons reach 5.07:1, which passes AA, and every image has alt text.

My role

  • Made three versions of the logo plus a moodboard and colour palette.
  • Illustrated the poster series in Adobe Illustrator.
  • Defined the target audience and wrote a user persona.
  • Built the site in WordPress with map plugins and an embedded YouTube video.
  • Audited the site for contrast and alt text problems and fixed them in code.
  • Rebuilt the site in HTML and CSS.

Audience and persona

The site's content could interest a wide range of ages, but the most important group for this project is 25 to 35 year olds. People in this age range are more likely to have money to give and time to engage with the topic, so targeting them gives the LCA the best chance of raising funds.

This age group is also more likely to understand the more controversial sides of habitat loss, such as poaching and the overdevelopment of residential areas, so the site can go into more depth. I then built a persona to represent this audience.

RG

User persona

Robert Gardner

Age 32 · Lawyer

Core needs

He wants facts on how people harm the natural world. He is interested in the topic but unsure of the facts and figures. He wants to feel like he can make a real difference to habitat loss. He only trusts charities that are open about their aims and do not make false promises.

Pain points

He thinks the media has been misleading about habitat loss and that its real causes are misunderstood. He is unhappy with his own complacency about the destruction of natural habitats. He finds a lot of charity websites insincere and hard to trust.

Design 1 is symmetrical, so it suited my first poster. For future posters I would use design 3, because it works better as a small logo in the corner.

My early experiments were quite extreme, so I toned down many of the design elements I was considering.

I think the main poster in the hero section is a successful result of this experimentation. The greyscale leopard contrasts well with the bold typography and gives the poster a more emotive feel.

Accessibility

I checked my colour palette against WCAG. White text on my near-black background scores 18.88:1.

When I audited my WordPress site I found two problems. The white text on my orange buttons only reached 4.14:1. That is below the 4.5:1 that WCAG AA needs for normal text. I had also forgotten to add alt text to my images.

In the coded version I made the button text black. This brought the contrast up to 5.07:1. On hover it goes up to 9.24:1 which also passes the stricter AAA level. Every image now has alt text. On the Spreading Awareness page the alt text includes the words on each poster.

Coded site

For the final part of the project I had to rebuild the WordPress site in HTML and CSS. I think the coded version is better in two ways. The navbar links and donate buttons have hover effects now, which I could not get working in WordPress. It also has the accessibility fixes above.

It is not a perfect copy. Some of the spacing and image sizes are different from the WordPress version. The masthead is the wrong size and on two pages the logo and title sit a bit differently in the navbar. I wrote these problems up in my submission.

Reflection

Overall the final WordPress site is a good match for how I wanted the LCA to come across to users. The audit taught me to test for accessibility earlier and more thoroughly so my work suits as wide an audience as possible.

Next time I would test the site with people from the target audience. That would show whether it builds the trust the brand is aiming for.
