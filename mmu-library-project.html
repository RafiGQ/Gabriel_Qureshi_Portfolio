Collaborative Library Redesign

A group project for the MMU Library. We interviewed students and tested a new prototype to fix the confusing labels in the library's menu. Some of our changes were adopted on the live site.

Focus
Information architecture, usability and design systems

The problem

The library's "Referencing and study support" menu used jargon like EndNote and Literature Reviews. Students did not recognise these terms so the section was hard to find your way around.

My actions

I put our group research together into the first Figma prototype and ran usability tests with MMU students. I also built interactive Figma components based on 3 user interviews and what the client told us.

Outcome

Using the interview feedback we relabelled and shortened the "Referencing and study support" drop-down menu. The library then adopted some of these changes on its live website.

My role

  • Co-led the prototype and built its responsive components in Figma.
  • Created interactive Figma components based on user feedback.
  • Built the design system as Figma variables with primitives, semantic tokens and light and dark modes.
  • Ran student usability tests as both interviewer and transcriber.
  • Kept the group's communication and deadlines on track.

Research methods

The project set out to fix problems in the MMU Library's "Referencing and study support" section. We used three methods to keep our findings accurate.

  1. Found issues on our own as a group.
  2. Checked those issues with the client in a meeting.
  3. Interviewed 3 MMU students. Each one did the same 3 tasks on the live site and then on our prototype.

We ran the interviews after building the prototype so our changes were based on what students needed and not on our own guesses.

Findings

We expected clearer labels to make the menu easier to use. The interviews backed this up. Students picked out the same confusing wording on the live site without us pointing it out.

Clear labels made navigation faster

Participants finished all 3 tasks noticeably faster on the prototype than on the live site. We observed this during the sessions rather than timing it. We think the simpler labels lowered their cognitive load.

Students rely on clear names

This was clearest when we compared the old menu titles with the new ones. Students struggled with titles that included software names like EndNote. None of the 3 students knew what EndNote was.

Changes after testing

"Tutorials and Workshops" became "Research Guides and Support"

Why
To give EndNote a proper section instead of leaving it on its own in the drop-down menu.
What we saw
None of the 3 participants knew what EndNote meant so they were less likely to use it.

"Literature Reviews" became "Search Skills/Literature Review"

Why
A plainer name for students who do not know the term. It still keeps "Literature Review" for those who do.
What we saw
2 of the 3 participants did not know the term or only had a vague idea. One was familiar with it.

Buttons became a drop-down menu

Why
The buttons looked too similar to the tabbed content function.
What we saw
Students found the drop-down menus simpler and easier to use, with less scrolling.

After these changes we updated the Figma prototype.

Design system
