Library Support Ticket Rework

A support ticket form that gives students a clearer and less daunting way to contact the library than writing an email.

Client
MMU Library (self-initiated proposal)
Focus
Service design and accessibility
My role
Prototype, accessibility checks and presentation
Tools
Figma, HTML, CSS
Timeline
2 weeks
On this page
  • My role
  • Pain points
  • Prototype overview
  • Reflection

The problem

One of the students we interviewed said they preferred "Contact us" to "Email us". Writing an email felt too personal and overwhelming. The department categories on the original support form were also vague.

My actions

With a partner I prototyped a support ticket system that fits MMU's design style and checked it against accessibility standards.

Outcome

We presented the proposal to MMU Library staff and got feedback on what worked and what did not. The response was really positive. Staff told us some of the problems we found were ones they were already working on. That was good to hear because it meant our research was on the right track.

My role

  • Co-designed the prototype in Figma and co-created its responsive components with my partner.
  • Checked the prototypes against accessibility standards and the design style used across the MMU website.
  • Co-wrote the presentation for MMU Library staff and presented it with my partner.

Pain points

Earlier user feedback showed that asking students to email puts a lot of pressure on them because it feels too personal. As a team we agreed a ticketing system would help. It would also help the library team stay organised as the number of queries grows.

We also thought the department categories were too vague. Students could pick the wrong option and that could slow down replies across the whole system.

Prototype overview

To make the form less confusing we turned the "Subject" field into an "Enquiry Title". When a student chooses a department, related topics appear in a side panel. This makes the form quicker to fill in and easier to learn.

There is also a coded version of our Figma design in HTML and CSS, with working drop-down menus.

Open the coded form

Reflection

This project built on our research from the Collaborative Library Redesign. The first project was about information architecture and this one was about service design. Doing both gave me experience in more than one area of UX.

Presenting to the library team was also good practice. Getting client feedback on prototypes is a normal part of working in industry.
